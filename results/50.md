Recommend a 12-week stream-aligned pilot using existing staff. Adding a coordination manager preserves the three queues; first test whether changing ownership reduces waiting.

**Hypothesis and assumptions.** Layer ownership and separate velocity targets encourage local completion while customer features wait. This is a hypothesis, not a measured cause. Assume one bounded customer journey can be selected, staff can be dedicated, and routine changes can receive cross-layer ownership without bypassing safety controls.

**Pilot charter.** The engineering leader sponsors the experiment; one product owner selects a journey with recurring, comparable features. Form a dedicated team with UI, API, database, testing, and operational capability. Reallocate existing capacity and explicitly reduce commitments elsewhere. Choose membership through consultation and skill coverage, not visibility or seniority alone. Existing managers retain career support; the pilot has one work queue and no competing layer assignments.

Current boundary: customer feature → UI queue → API queue → database queue. Experimental boundary: customer journey → one team accountable through production and support.

**Decision rights.** The product owner prioritizes customer outcomes. The team decides implementation and routine releases under existing controls. A named technical lead owns cross-layer consistency and escalation. The sponsor resolves shared-resource conflicts within two working days. Database specialists provide time-boxed coaching; retain specialist review for genuinely high-risk changes. Record exceptions and their waiting time. Keep pay and leveling unchanged during the experiment.

**Evaluation.** Before starting, reconstruct recent comparable features using one definition: accepted customer request to production. Verify how the reported six weeks was calculated. Track median and 85th-percentile lead time, blocked days, external handoffs, release failures, support load, and weekly workload/ownership feedback. Include unfinished work and results from remaining teams. Suspend separate velocity targets for pilot work.

At weeks 4 and 12, review evidence and recorded dissent. Proposed success thresholds: median lead time improves at least 30%, blocked days halve, and quality, workload, and remaining-team delivery do not materially worsen. Confirm guardrails against baseline before launch. Pause for safety breaches or sustained overload; narrow scope or restore boundaries. Expand only with sufficient comparable completions; otherwise extend measurement. Consider coordination capacity only if evidence identifies a remaining coordination bottleneck.

Guidance used: stream-aligned boundaries, enabling interactions, cognitive-load checks, explicit decision rights, reversible pilots, and delivery/employee-health validation from the designated skill and its organizational-design and decision-workflow references. Limits: no staffing, feature-volume, queue-time, or quality data were supplied. Pilot duration and thresholds are proposals; no approvals or outcomes are claimed.
