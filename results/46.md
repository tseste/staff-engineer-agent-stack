# Investment decision

Defer the deployment-portal mandate. Eight of twelve teams already deploy within ten minutes; two interviews cannot establish organization-wide demand. Prioritize a database-access experiment targeting the reported three-day wait. Assumptions: routine grants recur and existing policy permits automation. Queue time saved is not equivalent to labor saved.

## Six-week investment and adoption plan

1. **Weeks 1–2: establish demand.** Proposed lead: platform technical product owner; dependency owners: database operations and security. Interview all twelve teams, including fast deployers and non-users. Inspect four weeks of access requests: frequency, request-to-usable-access p50/p90, queue versus handling time, approval reasons, rework, support effort, and deployment blockers. Validate database access as the highest-value recurring constraint before building.
2. **Weeks 3–4: deliver one slice.** Automate routine, policy-eligible access to existing databases. Publish an API callable from existing workflows; add a minimal form only if discovery supports it. Preserve team-owned deployment pipelines.
3. **Weeks 5–6: voluntary pilot.** Recruit four affected teams, including previously uninterviewed teams. Provide onboarding and office hours. Observe failures and interview non-users weekly. Retain existing reviewed access routes. Expand after demonstrated value; reconsider a portal if evidence shows a shared navigation problem.

## Proposed access contract

`POST /access-requests`: authenticated identity, team, database, environment, requested role, expiry, and idempotency key. Validate ownership, least privilege, policy, and quotas. Return request ID, status URL, policy decision, and actionable failure reason; retries return the same request. Expiry or explicit revocation removes grants; changes revalidate policy. Keep versioned policies and audit actor, decision, timestamps, and outcome. Platform owns API failures; database operations owns grant failures; security owns policy exceptions, with scope, compensating controls, owner, and expiry.

## Decision gate

Proposed two-week pilot targets: eligible-request p90 under one hour, ≥95% completion, three of four teams voluntarily using it, and repeat use where demand recurs. Track support hours, sentiment, policy coverage, and cost/request against baseline. Stop or reshape for weak demand, persistent non-use after onboarding, poor economics, or unmet safety requirements; publish results before expansion.

## Guidance used and limitations

Applied the skill’s platform-product discovery, outcome contracts, API-first self-service, voluntary adoption, escape hatches, and explicit stop criteria, using its product-evidence and policy/observability references plus value-record and golden-path templates. Owners and targets are proposals, not assignments or achieved results. No repository, telemetry, request volume, budget, or approval policy was provided; implementation and investment sizing require the discovery evidence. No operations were performed.
