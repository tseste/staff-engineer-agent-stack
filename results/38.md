# Capacity recommendation

The current fleet cannot sustain peak traffic after losing one zone. Next quarter’s forecast peak is **800 × 1.30 = 1,040 requests/sec**.

Assume identical pods, linear scaling, unchanged request mix, balanced placement, successful redistribution, and available dependencies supporting aggregate throughput. The supplied 120 requests/sec/pod is the demonstrated latency-compliant ceiling; test boundary and duration are unknown. These are conditional estimates, not verified failover guarantees.

For N evenly distributed pods:

- Healthy capacity = 120N requests/sec.
- One-zone-loss safe capacity = 120(N/2) requests/sec.
- Minimum fleet for peak D = 2 × ceil(D/120).

| Configuration | Healthy capacity | Zone-loss capacity | Peak | Zone-loss headroom |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Current: 10 pods | 1,200 | 600 | 800 | −200 |
| Unchanged next quarter | 1,200 | 600 | 1,040 | −440 |
| Present minimum: 14 pods | 1,680 | 840 | 800 | 40 |
| Future minimum: 18 pods | 2,160 | 1,080 | 1,040 | 40 |
| Recommended: 20 pods | 2,400 | 1,200 | 1,040 | 160 |

Throughput figures are requests/sec. Healthy utilization rises from 66.7% to 86.7% with ten pods; healthy headroom conceals the failure deficit.

**Action:** provision at least 14 pods immediately and 18 before growth arrives. Prefer **20 pods, ten per zone**, before next quarter: nine survivors still supply 1,080 requests/sec if another pod is unavailable. This spare-pod allowance is a planning assumption, not a measured necessity.

Provisional operating target: peak ≤120(n−1), where n is pods per zone, reserving one survivor. Scale in balanced pairs when forecast peak within provisioning lead time exceeds this target; measure that lead time. Proposed owners: service engineering reviews forecasts weekly; platform engineering provisions capacity.

Validate end-to-end in representative staging: hold 1,040 requests/sec for 30 minutes, fail either zone, then remove one survivor; soak for 24 hours. Require existing latency/error SLOs, stable queues and memory, and no dependency saturation. Tests are proposed, not executed.

At constant pod cost, 14/18/20 pods cost 40%/80%/100% more than today; dollar costs require pricing.
