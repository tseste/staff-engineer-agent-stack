# Two-week cost reduction plan

Assume $60k is recurring monthly spend; pricing and retention obligations are unknown. Finance’s target is $24k/month savings, leaving $36k/month. Treat day 14 as a verified run-rate checkpoint; actual invoice savings depend on rollout timing and billing terms.

1. **Days 1–2 — FinOps and service owners: establish evidence.** Attribute compute, database, and logs costs by Environment, Owner, CostCenter, Project, and ManagedBy. Separate usage charges from existing commitments. Examine available peak-period history, per-resource CPU/memory, latency, errors, I/O, and log ingestion versus storage. Record baseline cost and service objectives; proceed despite incomplete tagging.

2. **Days 2–7 — Platform: prioritize compute.** Twelve percent average CPU warrants investigation, not a proportional capacity cut. Schedule confirmed nonproduction idle periods and stop owner-confirmed unused resources. Canary one sizing step or reduce excess replicas where peak resource demand and redundancy allow. Preserve autoscaling ceilings and minimum redundancy. Expand after a representative peak; immediately restore capacity on service-objective breaches, saturation, or queue growth. Check whether existing commitments prevent bill reductions.

3. **Days 3–9 — Observability owner: reduce log costs.** Suppress unnecessary debug/duplicate events and sample suitable high-volume streams. Propose shorter searchable retention with archive lifecycle policies that preserve required total retention. Obtain retention-owner approval before deletion; exclude mandatory audit records. Test retrieval and include archive, query, transition, and retrieval charges. Storage reductions will not eliminate ingestion charges; migrations may temporarily increase costs.

4. **Days 5–12 — Database owner: protect memory headroom.** Seventy percent peak memory does not justify downsizing. With unchanged demand, 25% less RAM would imply approximately 93% utilization. Investigate idle nonproduction databases, unnecessary replicas, queries, and storage first. Any resizing needs representative load/failover testing, validated backups, and tested reversal; retain availability requirements.

5. **Days 10–14 — FinOps: reconcile and report.** Track each change’s observed usage, incremental bill effect, confidence, rollout cost, and rollback status. Enable budget/anomaly alerts and weekly review. Defer long commitments until demand stabilizes; restrict spot capacity to interruption-tested workloads.

Eliminating all $10k of logs still requires $14k compute savings (47%) without database reductions. Forty percent is unproven: report verified savings and the gap, never a guaranteed estimate.
