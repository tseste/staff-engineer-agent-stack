The proposal can charge a customer repeatedly. A timeout means the outcome is unknown: the provider may have committed and lost the response. A new request ID makes the retry appear to be a new payment. Five retries after the initial call could produce six charges.

Assumptions: one logical payment permits at most one charge. The provider supports atomic idempotency, documented concurrent-request behavior and retention, and result recovery. Verify these capabilities.

Minimal robust design:

1. Before calling the provider, durably create a payment record containing a stable logical payment identifier, provider idempotency key, immutable amount/currency/payee, and `pending` status. Enforce uniqueness per logical payment: concurrent submissions and customer retries retrieve the same record. Reject changed details under the same key.
2. Reuse that key and payload on every attempt, including after restarts. Per-attempt tracing IDs may change. The provider must atomically associate the key with the charge and recover its result; local deduplication cannot protect the external effect.
3. Treat timeouts as `unknown`, never as proof of failure. Recover through provider lookup or documented same-key replay. Persist confirmed success or definitive failure. The provider is authoritative for charges; the local record tracks intent and reconciliation. Make success handling idempotent to prevent duplicate fulfillment.
4. Retry only documented transient failures, with per-call timeouts, exponential backoff and jitter, and shared attempt/deadline budgets across workers and restarts. Do not automatically retry declines or validation errors.
5. When budgets expire, retain an unresolved state and reconcile through bounded status checks or operator review. Never automatically generate a replacement key. Stop charge retries before deduplication expires. Without reliable provider deduplication or recovery, reconcile ambiguous outcomes before attempting another charge.

Proposed regression cases: committed charge with lost response; concurrent submissions; crash before local success persistence; duplicate success notifications; exhausted budget; expired key. Verify one charge, no duplicate fulfillment, and eventual correct state. No tests were executed.
