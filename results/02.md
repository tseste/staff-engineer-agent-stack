# Recommendation

Retain Django and evolve toward a modular monolith. Defer the proposed 12 services. The reported deployment failures arise from shared mutable test fixtures; service extraction does not remove that cause. Eight engineers would also inherit additional deployments, network failures, observability, and on-call responsibilities.

CTO: proposed accountable decision owner. Technical lead: proposed delivery owner. Stakeholders: engineers, operators, and customers. Review capacity evidence before next year's growth. Recommendation only; approval pending.

Assumptions: 10× growth means approximately 1,500 requests/sec with a comparable workload; preserving p95 ≤220ms and availability ≥99.95% is desirable. These are proposed targets, not established SLOs. Confirm measurement windows, traffic mix, peaks, and growth confidence.

Priorities and actions:

1. **Restore delivery reliability.** Backend and CI owners replace shared mutable fixtures with per-test factories, isolate database state and external resources, and reset mutable globals. Proposed acceptance: 100 consecutive representative CI runs, including randomized ordering and parallel execution, with zero fixture-related failures. Track all failures without excluding retries; passing does not prove permanent absence of flakiness.
2. **Establish capacity evidence.** Capacity and platform owners test representative traffic and realistic data volumes at 150, 750, and 1,500 requests/sec. Record latency, errors, database contention, resource saturation, and cost. Use profiling to prioritize query/index changes, caching, or additional application replicas. Current throughput alone establishes neither headroom nor a scaling ceiling.
3. **Improve boundaries incrementally.** Technical lead identifies cohesive business capabilities from change history and transaction scope. Assign each module a responsible engineer and backup, narrow interfaces, and exclusive write authority over its data. Assuming a relational database, retain local transactions and one deployable; enforce import and write-access boundaries in CI. Shared deployment/database failures remain accepted costs; bound dependency timeouts and avoid blind write retries.

Unchanged architecture is cheapest initially but leaves coupling unmanaged. Twelve services offer potential isolation and independent scaling, with unproven benefits and substantial operational costs.

Reconsider one extraction only when measured independent demand or release contention justifies it, ownership and data authority are clear, cross-boundary transactions are avoidable, and failure recovery is testable. Hand approved extraction to migration engineering with coexistence and rollback conditions.
