**Decision: Assess the distributed database** in Platforms & Infrastructure. Retain PostgreSQL for both teams. No measured scaling problem justifies replacement; regional reads are the concrete next-quarter outcome. A free year changes pricing, not technical suitability or migration risk.

Assumptions: PostgreSQL remains the write authority; the regional workload can tolerate bounded staleness. Proposed experiment targets are p95 regional reads ≤150 ms and p99 replication lag ≤1 second; the product owner must validate these before results guide a production decision. Strongly consistent reads would require different options and criteria.

**Experiment:** propose the regional team's tech lead as owner, with one engineer from each team and a database/SRE adviser. Timebox assessment to ten working days and twenty engineer-days; use isolated environments and synthetic data. Review at day ten, before committing the next-quarter delivery plan.

1. Define representative queries, transactions, data volume, and expected peak load; establish the current PostgreSQL baseline. Compare PostgreSQL with a regional read replica against the candidate database using identical workloads and regions. Run expected peak and twice-peak scenarios; record assumptions separately from measurements.
2. Measure read/write latency, lag, errors, correctness, and operator effort. Exercise regional disconnection, recovery, backup restoration, and export back into PostgreSQL. Record unsupported SQL, transaction differences, and application changes.
3. Estimate three-year costs, including renewal pricing, hosting, egress, migration, training, on-call support, and exit. Check licensing, support, operational maturity, security obligations, and vendor dependence; unknowns remain unknown.

**Exit criteria:** if PostgreSQL meets the validated targets, deliver regional reads with it and close replacement work. Advance the candidate to **Trial** only if PostgreSQL fails a required outcome, the candidate passes latency, consistency, recovery, and export checks without lost committed writes, and its three-year cost and operational burden have an accepted business case. Failed correctness or portability, unresolved critical risks, or inconclusive evidence means no promotion. Stop at the timebox; no automatic extension.

Use an advice process for assessment; record both teams' concerns and results. Production replacement requires centralized engineering-leader review because migration affects both teams. Exceptions need scope, owner, compensating controls, and expiry. Successful assessment cannot justify Adopt.
